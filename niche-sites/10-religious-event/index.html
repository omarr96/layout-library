Eid on the Meadow

Pray together in the open air at 8:30, then stay for a long, easy family day — breakfast stalls, a kids' corner, henna and a community stage. Free, and everyone is welcome.

The date depends on the moon sighting. We confirm it here and by text to everyone registered by 9 pm the evening before. If Eid falls on Monday 17 May, the whole programme simply moves to Monday.

The day

One morning prayer, then the whole day together

Come for the prayer, stay for breakfast, leave when the kids finally run out of energy. Everything happens within a short walk on the meadow.

  1. 7:45 am

    Gates open, mats go down

    Volunteers lay the main rows. Bring your own mat if you can — we have spares at the welcome tent.

  2. 8:30 am

    Eid prayer and sermon

    Led by Imam Yusuf Hadley. Brothers' rows face the oak line; the sisters' prayer area is screened, shaded and has its own entrance.

  3. 9:15 am

    Greetings and breakfast

    The food court opens: chai, fresh parathas, dates and pastries. Find your neighbours, take the family photo.

  4. 10:00 am

    Kids' corner and henna tent

    Bouncy castle for under-8s, face painting, craft table and a story circle. Henna artists work through the day.

  5. 11:30 am

    Community stage

    Nasheed groups, a youth poetry slot, local school choir and a short welcome from neighbouring faith groups.

  6. 2:00 pm

    Family games

    Sack race, three-legged race and the famously competitive parents-versus-teens tug of war.

  7. 4:00 pm

    Close and clean-up

    We leave the meadow cleaner than we found it. Grab a litter-picker if you can spare twenty minutes.

Food court

Fourteen local stalls, all halal and clearly labelled for nuts and dairy. Most dishes $3–$9, and free chai until 10.

Kids' corner

Fenced, shaded and staffed by checked volunteers. Under-8s need a grown-up nearby; wristbands at the gate.

Henna & crafts

Three henna artists from 10 am, $5 a hand with proceeds to the Centre's food bank. Walk-ups only.

Bring the family. Bring the neighbours too.

Eid on the Meadow is open to everyone in Northgate, whatever your faith. Last year more than 2,400 people came along — about a third of them for the first time.

Getting there

Find us on the Great Meadow

Hollins Park, 300 Elm Street, Northgate. Parking is limited, so walk, bus or take our free shuttle from the Centre if you can.

Bus & shuttle

Bus 14 stops at Elm Street Gate. Our free shuttle runs from Northgate Islamic Centre every 15 minutes from 7:15 am, and back from 12 pm.

Parking

Park lot on Mill Road, $5 for the day, cash or card. 24 accessible bays held near the gate — show your placard to a steward.

Access

Firm matting from the gate to both prayer areas, chairs for anyone who prays seated, two accessible restrooms and a quiet tent by the pond.
