When someone dies

There's no need to decide anything tonight

These are the only things that need to happen in the first few hours. Everything else can wait until you're ready.

Available 24 hours, every day(802) 555-0148

A funeral director answers, never a call center. We usually arrive within two hours.

  1. Confirm the death

    If your loved one was in hospice care at home, call the hospice nurse. If the death was unexpected, call 911. In a hospital or care home, the staff will do this for you.

  2. Call us, at any hour

    Tell us where your loved one is and we'll come to bring them gently into our care. You're welcome to take time to say goodbye first; there's no rush.

    If you haven't chosen a funeral home yet, it's fine to call us just to ask questions.

  3. Rest, and gather family

    We'll arrange to meet the next day or later, at your home or here. It helps to bring any written wishes, a recent photo, and clothing if you'd like them dressed.

  4. We'll handle the paperwork

    Death certificates, permits, Social Security notification, veterans' honors, newspaper notices and the online obituary. You'll get a simple checklist of what's left for the family.

Planning ahead

Questions

Things families often ask

Is embalming required?
No. Embalming is not required by law except in certain special cases. If you'd like a viewing, we'll talk through whether it helps. For direct cremation or immediate burial, it's never needed.
Can we see the prices before we commit to anything?
Of course. You'll receive our General Price List at the start of any arrangement meeting, and you can ask for prices by phone at any time. After the meeting you'll get a written statement of exactly what you chose.
How soon can a funeral take place?
Usually within four to seven days, depending on clergy, cemetery and family travel. Cremation needs a permit from the state medical examiner, which normally takes one to two business days.
Do you help with veterans' benefits?
Yes. We arrange military honors, a burial flag and a government headstone or marker, and help families apply for burial allowances. Please bring a copy of the DD-214 if you have one.
What if we can't afford a funeral?
Please call us anyway. We'll explain the most affordable options, help you apply for state General Assistance burial support, and never pressure you toward anything you don't need.
