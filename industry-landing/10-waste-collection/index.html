What goes where

The bin guide, in plain English

Rinse it, squash it, keep it loose. If you're unsure, put it in general waste: one wrong item can spoil a whole lorry of recycling.

Blue wheelie bin

Paper and cardboard, flattened. Baled at our yard and sold to a paper mill in Kent.

Collected every week

Yes please

  • Newspapers, magazines, junk mail
  • Cereal boxes and egg boxes
  • Delivery boxes, tape removed
  • Envelopes, even with windows
  • Wrapping paper without glitter

No thanks

  • Greasy pizza boxes (general waste)
  • Shredded paper loose (bag it)
  • Coffee cups and Tetra Paks
  • Foil or glitter wrapping
  • Tissues and kitchen roll

Green box

Glass bottles and jars, tins, cans, aerosols and clean foil. Lids on, labels can stay.

Collected every week

Yes please

  • Wine, beer and sauce bottles
  • Jam and baby-food jars
  • Food and drink cans
  • Empty aerosols
  • Clean foil trays, scrunched

No thanks

  • Drinking glasses and Pyrex
  • Window glass or mirrors
  • Light bulbs (take to a tip)
  • Paint tins with paint left
  • Batteries (use the bag on top)

Food caddy & brown bin

Food goes to an anaerobic digester near Bristol that powers 3,000 homes. Garden waste becomes compost.

Food weekly · garden fortnightly

Yes please

  • All cooked and raw food, bones too
  • Tea bags and coffee grounds
  • Grass, leaves, hedge cuttings
  • Small branches under 10cm
  • Cut flowers and weeds

No thanks

  • Plastic bags, even "compostable"
  • Soil, rubble and turf
  • Pet waste or cat litter
  • Packaging around food
  • Treated wood

Black wheelie bin

What's left. Sent to an energy-from-waste plant, so even this avoids landfill.

Collected every 2 weeks

Yes please

  • Crisp packets and film plastic
  • Nappies and sanitary waste, bagged
  • Polystyrene
  • Broken crockery, wrapped
  • Greasy paper and cardboard

No thanks

  • Batteries and vapes (fire risk)
  • Electricals of any size
  • Hot ashes
  • Rubble or soil
  • Anything that fits in recycling

FAQ

Before you book

Still stuck? Call 0117 496 0321, Mon–Fri 7am–6pm, Sat 8am–1pm.

Do I need a permit for a skip on the road?
Yes, if it can't go on your drive. We apply to the council for you; permits cost from £45 for 14 days and take about two working days, so tell us early.
What happens if I overfill the skip?
Waste must sit level with the top edge so it can be sheeted safely. If it's over, the driver will ask you to remove the excess or charge £30 for us to take it off.
Can I keep the skip longer than 14 days?
Of course. Extra days are £6 each, or swap a full skip for an empty one for the same price as a new booking minus £20.
What if my bin is missed?
Report it by text or online before 8pm and a crew will return within 24 hours. If it was our mistake, that month's fee is reduced by a quarter.
Are you a licensed waste carrier?
Yes. We're registered upper-tier carriers and run a permitted transfer station. Every customer gets a Waste Transfer Note, which businesses are legally required to keep for two years.
