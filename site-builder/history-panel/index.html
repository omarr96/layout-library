How history behaves

    
One rule drives everything: moving around history never changes it, and nothing is deleted without a way back. Clicking a step only moves you to it. Only a real edit or a restore adds a new entry.


    

      

        
01
Clicking an older step

        

        
The page returns to how it looked after that step. Later steps turn grey but stay in the list, so you can click them or press ⇧⌘Z to go forward again. No new entry is added. The canvas shows a "Viewing an earlier step" bar with a "Back to latest" button.

      

      

        
02
Editing from an older step

        

        

        
The edit becomes a new entry right after the step you're on, and it is now the latest step. The greyed steps are not deleted, unlike Photoshop's default. They fold into a "Branch · 2 actions" row you can open and switch back to.

      

      

        
03
Restoring a saved version

        

        
First, your current page is auto-saved as "Before restore". The restore then adds one Actions entry, which a single ⌘Z undoes. The page stays unsaved until you save, so newer versions are never overwritten. This follows Figma, which keeps a checkpoint before restoring.

      

      

        
04
Typing, dragging and saving

        

        
Keystrokes in the same field less than 1.5 s apart become one entry, and so does one slider drag, showing before → after. Saving adds a "Saved" marker and does not clear Actions. Reloading clears Actions; Versions stay.
