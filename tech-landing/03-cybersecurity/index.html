What actually goes wrong

Attackers don't break in. They log in.

Most attacks on small businesses aren't clever. They use a stolen password, a convincing email or an unpatched laptop. Here's what we see most often and what we do about each one.

01

Invoice fraudBusiness email compromise

What it looks like

An attacker gets into a mailbox, watches quietly for weeks, then sends your customer "updated bank details" on a real invoice thread.

What we do

We flag odd sign-ins and new forwarding rules within seconds, lock the account and check every message that left while they were inside.

02

RansomwareFiles locked, business stopped

What it looks like

Usually starts hours or days before the ransom note: a new admin account, a remote-access tool nobody installed, backups quietly deleted.

What we do

We catch those early steps and cut the infected machine off the network, while everyone else keeps working.

03

Stolen loginsMicrosoft 365, Google, VPN

What it looks like

A password leaked from another site, or a fake login page that also steals the MFA code. To your systems it looks like a normal sign-in.

What we do

We learn how each person normally works and step in when a login doesn't fit: wrong place, impossible travel, a new device at 2 a.m.

04

The one clickPhishing & human error

What it looks like

A busy employee opens a "shared document" or approves a sign-in prompt they didn't start. It happens to careful people.

What we do

Short monthly training based on real lures we've seen, plus a report button. When someone does click, we're already watching what happens next.
